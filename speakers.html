Co-ordinators of the CIVIS BIP Course

A distinguished international faculty bridging the gap between historical philology and modern computational science.

Professor Nikolaos Lavidas

Professor of Diachronic Linguistics

National and Kapodistrian University of Athens

Professor Nikolaos Lavidas is a leading expert in the Department of English Language and Literature. His scholarly work focuses on historical linguistics with a specialized interest in the diachrony of Indo-European languages. As the scientific coordinator of the Athens Diachronic & Geometric Corpus (AthDGC), he has pioneered research into the evolution of syntax and argument structure. His publications explore the intricate relationship between language contact and structural change. Within the CIVIS framework, he leads the scientific design and academic coordination of Blended Intensive Programmes in linguistics.

Core Research Areas

Diachronic Syntax, Language Contact, Indo-European Philology, Corpus Design, and Argument Structure Evolution.

Professor Francesca Dell’Oro

Associate Professor

University of Bologna

Professor Francesca Dell’Oro specializes in Ancient Greek and Indo-European comparative linguistics. Her research is at the forefront of digital humanities, where she applies computational methods to epigraphic and onomastic data. She has conducted extensive studies on the Styra tablets and the diachronic development of local Greek dialects. Her work provides critical insights into the evolution of the Greek language through the lens of ancient inscriptions and historical manuscripts.

Core Research Areas

Greek Onomastics, Epigraphy, Digital Humanities, and Comparative Indo-European Linguistics.

Professor Artemij Keidan

Associate Professor of Linguistics

Sapienza Università di Roma

Professor Artemij Keidan teaches General Linguistics and Sanskrit at Sapienza. His research bridges the divide between traditional grammatical traditions and contemporary computational modeling. He is a primary advocate for "multistratal" approaches to linguistics, exploring how Artificial Intelligence can assist in the structural analysis and formalization of historical texts. His work often focuses on the formalization of ancient grammatical rules and their application in modern NLP environments.

Core Research Areas

AI in Linguistics, Sanskrit Philology, Computational Modeling of Grammar, and Linguistic Theory.

Professor Ljuba Veselinova

Professor of Linguistics

Stockholm University

Professor Ljuba Veselinova is an internationally recognized typologist focusing on language change, grammaticalization, and the diachrony of specific grammatical categories. Her research into negation systems—specifically the Jespersen and Croft cycles—is widely cited in diachronic studies. She utilizes large-scale cross-linguistic databases to identify universal patterns in how languages evolve over time, contributing significantly to our understanding of the lifecycle of grammatical structures.

Core Research Areas

Language Typology, Grammaticalization, Diachrony of Negation, and Cross-linguistic Data Analysis.

Professor Maria Isabel Jimenez Martinez

Professor of Ancient and Modern Greek

Universidad Autónoma de Madrid

Professor Maria Isabel Jimenez Martinez is a specialist in the diachronic development of Greek and Latin. Her research focus lies in the transition from Latin to the Romance languages, with a particular interest in verb-nominal collocations and their stability across historical periods. She integrates classical philology with modern syntactic analysis, providing a comparative framework that connects ancient linguistic roots to modern Mediterranean language structures.

Core Research Areas

Latin and Romance Diachrony, Comparative Syntax, Verb-Nominal Collocations, and Medieval Philology.

Professor Tamara Bouso Rivas

Researcher & Faculty Member

University of Santiago de Compostela

Professor Tamara Bouso Rivas focuses on English historical linguistics through the lens of cognitive and constructionist theories. Her work is deeply rooted in corpus-based methods, investigating the development of complex syntactic structures in English. She is a prominent expert in Construction Grammar (CxG), specifically analyzing how "Objoid" constructions and other syntactic patterns emerge and shift diachronically within the English language family.

Core Research Areas

Construction Grammar, English Historical Syntax, Corpus Linguistics, and Cognitive Diachronic Analysis.
