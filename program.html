Phase 1: Intensive Digital Foundations

20-24 April 2026 (Online)

Week-Long Intensive Spring School

Immerse yourself in cutting-edge computational historical linguistics with expert-led instruction and interactive learning.

  • Computational Historical Linguistics Intensive

    Master AI-powered tools and techniques for linguistic analysis. Learn how to leverage machine learning algorithms and NLP tools for historical text processing.

  • Expert-Led Lectures

    Learn from pioneers in digital humanities and language evolution. Engage with leading researchers from CIVIS universities on cutting-edge topics in diachronic linguistics.

  • Interactive Masterclasses

    Develop advanced methodological skills for describing, analyzing, and explaining language change through computational approaches. Hands-on sessions with real linguistic data.

  • Introduction to Digital Corpora

    Explore major historical linguistic databases and learn corpus creation and annotation techniques.

Learning Format

All sessions will be conducted online via video conferencing platforms. Interactive elements include live Q&A, breakout rooms for group work, and hands-on coding exercises. Recordings will be available for those who cannot attend synchronously.

Phase 2: Applied Project Work

May 2026 (Online)

Collaborative Workshops & Mini-Projects

Put theory into practice through hands-on digital workshops. Work directly with instructors and international peers on real computational linguistics projects.

  • Collaborative Online Workshops

    Weekly synchronous sessions to work with instructors and fellow students. Dates to be announced after the Spring School.

  • 'From Texts to Grammar' Mini-Projects

    Apply computational tools to real historical texts in guided project work:

    • Build custom NLP pipelines for historical language processing
    • Create digital grammatical models from raw textual data
    • Use machine learning to track linguistic patterns across centuries
    • Develop your own computational analysis of language development
  • Individual/Team Project Development

    Work independently or in small groups on your chosen research project with ongoing instructor support.

  • Peer Review Sessions

    Present work-in-progress to fellow students and receive constructive feedback.

Project Options

Students can choose from various project types based on their interests and background:

  • Corpus analysis projects
  • Digital text analysis with computational tools
  • Collaborative case studies in language change
  • Development of annotation schemes for historical texts

Phase 3: Immersive Summer Experience

20-24 July 2026 (Naxos, Greece)

40 Hours of Face-to-Face Learning on Naxos

Where Digital Innovation Meets Mediterranean Inspiration

Complete your interdisciplinary journey with five transformative days on the stunning island of Naxos, Cyclades, where ancient heritage provides the perfect backdrop for cutting-edge computational linguistics.

  • Monday-Friday: Daily Intensive Sessions

    8 hours per day of concentrated learning and project work

    • Morning sessions: Lectures and demonstrations
    • Afternoon sessions: Hands-on workshops and project work
    • Evening sessions: Networking and informal discussions
  • Collaborative Research Labs

    Work alongside international peers and leading experts in dedicated computational linguistics facilities. Real-time problem-solving with immediate expert feedback.

  • Project Presentations

    Present your completed mini-projects to the group. Choose from poster presentations, team research presentations, or methodological demonstrations.

  • Keynote Lectures

    Special lectures from distinguished visitors and CIVIS faculty on cutting-edge topics in computational historical linguistics.

  • Networking & Innovation Sessions

    Connect with the next generation of digital humanities researchers. Build lasting international collaborations.

Mobility Grant

Selected students will be supported by an Erasmus+ grant for physical mobility to Greece.

The grant helps cover:

  • Travel expenses to and from Naxos
  • Accommodation during the summer school
  • Course materials and facilities

Note: Grant allocation is competitive and based on academic merit, motivation, and financial need.
